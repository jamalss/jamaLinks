Root, branch, and crown

Meekness

A leaf does not fight the season. It turns brilliant, then lets go — patient, unresisting, still rooted to the very end. Scripture calls that meekness.

Part One

The Root

Before meekness can be seen in a life, it has to be understood as a word. This is where it starts — buried in Hebrew soil, centuries before the Sermon on the Mount.

Meekness is the quality of being patient in the reception of injuries; it stands opposed to malice and revenge.

The leaf that lets go is not defeated by the wind — it was always going to fall well, because it never held on by force. Meekness works the same way: it is not surrender, it is trust, worn as a posture toward God and toward everyone else.

Meekness — the quality of being patient in the reception of injuries.
